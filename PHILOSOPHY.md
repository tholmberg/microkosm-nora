# Microkosm Labs Philosophy

## Build technology people can understand, own, and control.

Microkosm Labs exists for people who want a real relationship with their technology: not just to consume it, but to understand it, question it, experiment with it, and make it their own.

We believe powerful tools should be accessible without demanding that people surrender their privacy, identity, or agency. We favor local-first computing, open standards, inspectable behavior, and systems that work on hardware their users control.

**Ownership.** Your hardware, your data, your credentials, your decisions. Remote services should be optional where practical, and dependencies should be visible.

**Transparency.** People deserve to know what software is doing, what it can access, what it cannot do, and why it took an action. An attempted operation is not the same as a verified outcome.

**Experimentation.** Curiosity belongs in the product. Give people safe ways to test, break, rebuild, and extend their tools, with meaningful boundaries and reversible actions.

**Education.** Documentation, examples, and guided projects are part of the software, not an afterthought. Every Microkosm Labs project should teach something useful.

**Privacy and autonomy.** Collect as little as practical. Keep authority with the user. No assistant should silently grant itself permissions, and convenience should not require surrendering control.

**Interoperability.** Build for open protocols and replaceable components, not dependence on one vendor, assistant, or cloud. Challenge unnecessary lock-in while respecting other people's rights and security.

## How we put this into practice

- **Taxi Driver** teaches operational visibility and control: connect tools, inspect health, grant scoped permissions, verify outcomes, and learn through guided projects. It is an independent operations platform, not an AI personality.
- **Nora** teaches local AI and human-machine collaboration: reason, explain, and recommend while leaving operational authorization to the user and the systems they configure.
- **Data Quest** teaches analytics by letting people experiment, make mistakes, and learn through play.

We should judge our work by more than whether a feature runs. Can someone understand it? Can they inspect its effects? Can they choose what it touches? Can they learn from it and leave with more control than they started with?

**We don't believe convenience should require surrendering control.**

This is a working philosophy, not a claim that every project already meets every principle. We intend to make the software earn these words.
